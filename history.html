A Breed with a Rich History


From medieval hunting fields to modern family homes — the remarkable journey of the Cocker Spaniel.

Origins . Timeline . Breeds

Origins

Born to Fetch

The Cocker Spaniel's story begins in Spain — the name "spaniel" is widely believed to derive from Espagnol, the old French word for Spanish. These dogs were prized hunting companions across Europe as far back as the 14th century, mentioned in texts alongside nobility and falconers.

By the 17th century, spaniels had spread across Britain, selectively bred into smaller, nimble dogs capable of flushing woodcock from dense undergrowth. It is this bird — the woodcock — that gave the Cocker its name.

The breed was formally recognised by The Kennel Club in 1892, cementing its status as a distinct and celebrated type.

Timeline

Key Moments through the Centuries

  • 14th Century

    Earliest Written Records

    Spaniels appear in European literature and manuscripts as versatile hunting dogs used alongside nets and falcons. Their instinct to flush birds from cover made them indispensable to hunters.

  • 1620

    Arriving in the New World

    A spaniel is recorded among the dogs that arrived on the Mayflower, making it one of the first breeds to set paw in America — long before formal breed registries existed.

  • 1800s

    Selective Breeding Takes Shape

    British breeders began distinguishing between larger "Field Spaniels" and smaller "Cockers." Dogs under 25 lbs were classified as Cockers, valued for working tight woodland cover where larger dogs struggled.

  • 1878

    American Kennel Club Recognition

    The American Kennel Club formally recognised the Cocker Spaniel, reflecting the breed's popularity that had crossed the Atlantic. American lines would later diverge significantly from British ones.

  • 1892

    The Kennel Club Recognition

    The Kennel Club in the UK officially registered the Cocker Spaniel as a distinct breed, separating it from other spaniel types and establishing breed standards for the first time.

  • 1930 - 1940

    America's Favourite Dog

    The American Cocker Spaniel rose to become the most popular dog in the United States for over 16 consecutive years — a record unmatched by any other breed at the time.

  • 1946

    Two Breeds Officially Recognised

    After decades of diverging breeding practices, the English Cocker Spaniel and American Cocker Spaniel were formally recognised as two separate breeds — a distinction that holds to this day.

  • Today

    A Beloved Family Companion

    While still used as working gundogs, the Cocker Spaniel is now cherished worldwide as a devoted family pet — consistently ranking among the most popular breeds in the UK and beyond.
